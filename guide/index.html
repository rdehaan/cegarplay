Designing CEGAR loops

A course in building counterexample-guided loops in answer set programming: what goes in the abstraction, how a counterexample becomes a refinement, how a refinement goes wrong, and which loop to choose.

What this guide is

New to both the guide and the playground? Follow the combined reading order; unsure of a term, see the glossary.

The guide teaches the design of a loop, not the use of the tool. For a tour of the playground's tabs, take the Tutorial; for the loop language and every option, look in the Manual.

Part I of this book teaches the design of a loop. Part II is a tour of the playground, the browser application in which every example of Part I runs. A glossary and a bibliography close Part I.

You should know answer set programming: choice rules, constraints, #show, and running clingo. You need not know anything about CEGAR.

How the links work

Every step of the guide is an example of the playground. A link marked Open loads the example in the playground and shows the program the text talks about. All such links use the same browser tab, so the guide stays where it is. In the playground, press Run; the link "Back to the guide" at the top left brings you back to the paragraph you came from. Loading an example does not replace the project you are editing in the playground until you change it.

The numbers on the pages (candidates, counterexamples, solutions) were measured with the same engine the playground uses, and a test keeps them up to date: your run shows the same numbers.

Part II: Loops from the literature

Each chapter starts from a problem and a blocking loop and derives a published algorithm in steps, with a box that maps the paper's pseudocode onto the loop script.

  1. 132QBF, by clauses and by expansion The loop of Part I is a known QBF algorithm; expansion learns a copy of the formula instead of a clause.
  2. 14Implicit hitting sets MaxSAT as an optimal hitting set of cores: lower and upper bounds, and minimal cores.
  3. 15MARCO: two kinds of answer A map of all subsets, shrink and grow, and refinements in both directions.
  4. 16CEGIS: synthesis from counterexamples The counterexample is an input; the synthesiser learns to be right on it.
  5. 17Core-guided optimisation: OLL One solver whose cores relax the problem instead of cutting it, against the hitting-set loop.
  6. 18Benders decomposition Master and subproblem, optimality cuts as rules, and how much a cut should cover.
  7. 19More than two quantifier levels Chains of solvers, recursion over solver families, and cores along the chain.
  8. 20Lazy constraints and omission Checks at partial assignments, and abstractions that leave part of the problem out.
  9. 21A case study: explaining voting outcomes A research project with the tool: strategies as variants, a campaign, ground truth and open questions.
  10. 22From a paper to a loop A checklist from pseudocode to loop script, and which loop for which problem.

How-to pages

Short pages with one goal each: how to use the playground's tools to answer a design question.

  • How to compare refinement strategies with a campaign: Variants, a suite, the instance × variant matrix and the plots.
  • How to find where the time goes: Solvers, candidates, clause lengths, the timeline and the cost per line.
  • How to hunt an unsound refinement: The audit, the candidate space, Fuzz and a regression instance.
  • How to build an instance set: Generators, imports, ground truth and Run all.
  • How to run experiments headlessly: run.mjs, bench.mjs, report.mjs, and when to move to native clingo.
  • How to check a loop independently: The reference variant, cegarcheck.py and proof logs with certcheck.py.
  • How to write a domain view: Scene and frame, the step facts, and checking a view against the trace.
  • How to record and present a run: Recordings, replays, storyboards and lecture links.
  • How to turn a paper into a loop: A checklist from pseudocode to loop script, and what to do when CegarPlay lacks something.
