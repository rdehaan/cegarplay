How to find where the time goes

Goal: a loop is slow. Is the check too slow, are there too many candidates, or are the refinements too weak?

The example

guide-05-paley-block and guide-05-paley-clause solve the same problem on a larger graph, one by blocking and one with a clause. Open the blocking loop

Steps

  1. Statistics tab, Solvers. Per solver: calls, solving time (without nested calls) and time per call. If the check's time per call dominates, the check is the problem; if the abstraction's calls are few but long, its search is.
  2. Count the candidates. Candidates against solutions: if most candidates are refuted, the refinements are too weak (or the abstraction knows too little, chapter 7).
  3. Refinements and the Lifting coach. The clause length distribution: long clauses are weak. With lifting on (manual, "Refinement variants"), the Lifting coach in the Statistics tab shows per interface predicate which atoms relevant/1 marked and which lifting kept.
  4. Observatory, timeline. Turn on Record calls (Loop tab), run again, and open the Observatory (Advanced mode). One lane per solver; long bars are slow calls.
  5. Observatory, cost per line. One row per line of the loop script, with calls and exclusive time; click a row to filter the timeline. The loop editor shows the same figures as annotations.

Deciding

  • Many candidates, short clauses, cheap checks: the refinement is fine but the problem is big; try a stronger abstraction (chapter 7) or several counterexamples per check (chapter 6).
  • Many candidates, long clauses: mark fewer atoms relevant, soundly (chapter 3), or minimise cores (chapter 14).
  • Few candidates, slow check calls: the check's encoding is the problem; try it alone with Export for clingo.
  • Time in the abstraction after many refinements: a Shape 2 loop regrounds or restarts; compare shapes (chapter 8).

Pitfalls

  • Recording calls costs time itself; compare timings with recording off.
  • A single run's time is noisy; Rerun with 5 seeds in the run history, or a campaign.
